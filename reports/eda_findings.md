- **Highest fatality rate by city — Pune · 15.8%** [not significant]. 398 of 2,517 accidents were fatal (95% CI 14.4–17.3%), versus 14.0% for Hyderabad. However the spread across cities is not statistically significant (p = 0.448) — it is consistent with random variation.
- **Most common cause — distraction** [not significant]. 4,026 accidents (20.1%), next: overspeeding (4,025). But counts across causes are statistically indistinguishable from an even split (p = 0.961), so 'distraction' leads only marginally.
- **Highest fatality rate by cause — poor road · 15.7%** [weak]. 623 of 3,974 accidents were fatal (95% CI 14.6–16.8%), versus 13.5% for distraction. Evidence of a difference is weak (p = 0.045, Cramér's V = 0.022): it does not survive correction for the many comparisons made here, so treat it as a lead, not a finding.
- **Most accident-prone time of day — Night** [not significant]. 840 accidents per clock-hour vs 819 for Morning (buckets are normalised by length: Night 8 h, Morning 7 h, Afternoon 5 h, Evening 4 h). Busiest single hour: 02:00 (888 accidents). The hourly distribution is statistically flat (p = 0.471), so no time period is meaningfully more accident-prone.
- **Most common weather condition — clear** [not significant]. 6,690 accidents (33.5%), next: rain (6,677). But counts across weather conditions are statistically indistinguishable from an even split (p = 0.875), so 'clear' leads only marginally. Without the share of hours each weather condition actually occurred, this cannot say that any weather makes accidents more likely.
- **Highest fatality rate by weather condition — fog · 15.0%** [not significant]. 995 of 6,633 accidents were fatal (95% CI 14.2–15.9%), versus 14.8% for clear. However the spread across weather conditions is not statistically significant (p = 0.955) — it is consistent with random variation.
- **Highest fatality rate by road type — urban · 15.4%** [not significant]. 1,039 of 6,745 accidents were fatal (95% CI 14.6–16.3%), versus 14.5% for rural. However the spread across road types is not statistically significant (p = 0.362) — it is consistent with random variation.
- **Weekend vs weekday — 16.6 vs 16.7 per day** [not significant]. Weekends average 16.64 accidents per calendar day vs 16.66 on weekdays (raw totals would mislead: 2 vs 5 days a week). Fatality rate 15.1% vs 14.9% (difference +0.2 pp, p = 0.717, not significant).
- **Peak vs off-peak hours — 825 vs 836 per hour** [not significant]. Peak hours in this data are 08, 09, 10, 17, 18, 19:00. Per clock-hour, peak and off-peak see 825 and 836 accidents. Fatality rate 15.0% vs 14.9% (p = 0.963).
- **Highest average risk score by cause — overspeeding · 0.440** [not significant]. Range across causes: 0.429–0.440 (Kruskal–Wallis p = 0.157). Cause is not an input to risk_score — the score is an exact additive rule of severity, traffic density, peak hour, weather, visibility and festival — so any gap between causes only reflects how those conditions happen to be mixed.
- **Festival-day accidents — 115 records** [unreliable]. Holi 38 accidents, 34.2% fatal (CI 21–50%, p = 0.002); Eid 34 accidents, 17.6% fatal (CI 8–34%, p = 0.630); Diwali 31 accidents, 9.7% fatal (CI 3–25%, p = 0.613); New Year 12 accidents, 0.0% fatal (CI 0–24%, p = 0.235). Non-festival fatality rate: 14.9%. Rates for New Year rest on fewer than 30 records and cannot be calculated reliably. Festival labels exist only for 2023–2024; other festival days are unlabelled.
- **Casualties by severity — Fatal 3.00 · Major 1.49 · Minor 1.51** [descriptive]. Average casualties per accident. Fatal accidents average 2.0× the casualties of major/minor ones; major vs minor differ by 0.01. 1,725 'major' accidents record zero casualties, so the severity label should be read as recorded, not as an injury scale.
